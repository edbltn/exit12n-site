What to make of Exit 12N

Exit 12N is the northbound off-ramp from the Henry Hudson Parkway to West 125th Street. It sits on parkland between the parkway and Riverside Park, near the 119th Street tennis courts and St. Clair Place, above an Amtrak tunnel. It closed to cars in July 2025. Walkers, cyclists, skateboarders and birders have used it since.

Transportation Alternatives, Spherical Harmonics and Open Plans are asking together whether it could go back to the community, as a place to walk and gather or as green space that helps the riverside withstand a changing climate. Neighbors were there first: the community board’s parks committee heard their plan in February 2025.

An online workshop on November 4 gave the map its first public test. On November 22 the three partners held a walk to the ramp, then a workshop at Columbia’s Center for Smart Streetscapes. The north end proved the hard part: it is steep and has no ramp, so people in wheelchairs or walking bikes must detour. People proposed composting, basketball, a bike lane through to 125th Street, separate paths for walkers and cyclists, a learn-to-ride area, a pollinator meadow, film screenings, bouldering on the cliff and a fishing pier.

What the map gathers goes to two Community Board 9 committees in December. The map decides nothing; it shows what people would like, so that those who decide, and everyone else, can look at it together. To see the proposals or add your own, open the map and vote.

The question

The off-ramp from the Henry Hudson Parkway at the foot of Riverside Park has been closed to cars since July 2025. Neighbors have been walking and biking into the park through it ever since. What should it become?

§1

The ramp Where things stand

Felipe Castillo Trujillo, Ira Gershenhorn and Transportation Alternatives’ Anna Berlanga had been organizing for it long before we arrived. Neighbors keep a website about it and a petition is open, and the community board's parks committee heard the neighbors' plan in February 2025.

Not everyone wants the same thing: some neighbors would like to see the ramp reopened to cars, others would like it to stay closed to cars and open to people on foot and on bikes. The community board's parks committee has said it will not take a position until it has heard both sides.

§2

The map Draw a proposal, vote on others

Exit 12N is a map you can use on a phone or a laptop. It shows the ramp and the streets and the park around it in 3D, as they are today. You can place something on it, such as a garden, a bench, a ball court or a bike lane, or draw a path, and you can add your vote to what others have drawn. A drawing with many votes behind it is easy to see, and so is one that only a few people want.

The map does not decide anything. It shows what people would like to see, so that the people who do decide, and everyone else, can look at it together.

To add your voice, open the map, find a drawing and press the plus or the minus. You do not need an account. If you want to draw something of your own, use the add button on the map and choose what to put down. You can take a vote back later.

Representatives Who decides, and how to write to them

The map shows what neighbors would like. The people who decide about the ramp are elected officials and the city's transportation department, and they hear from you directly too. Write whatever you think about the ramp, whether you want it kept as it is, changed or reopened. We do not tell you what to say.

The transportation department named its contact for the ramp in its July 2025 closure notice. For other city services, call 311.

A few things make a letter easier to act on:

  • Say who you are and which neighborhood you live in, and give your contact details.
  • Name the place exactly: the Henry Hudson Parkway northbound Exit 12N ramp to West 125th Street, at Riverside Park.
  • Say what you have seen, when and how often, and what it means for you. Photos help.
  • Ask for something specific: a review, the next steps, or which agency decides.
§5

Story How this came about, and what comes next

Neighbors were working on Exit 12N long before there was a map. In February 2025, months before the ramp closed, Felipe Castillo Trujillo brought Community Board 9’s parks committee a proposal to give one lane of the ramp to people on foot and on bikes. In July 2025 the ramp closed to cars. For about a year almost no one complained, until people found out it had closed. As Felipe puts it, the neighborhood does not need an exit for cars there.

Since late 2025 Ira Gershenhorn has kept a running record of the ramp on his own website: the trash he reported, the cleanups, the dogwood, Solomon’s seal, daffodils and wildflowers he planted by the gate, Car Free Earth Day, and what the Riverside Park Conservancy and Amtrak are doing on the land. Allegra LeGrande, who volunteers alongside him, says people call him “NYC Glue” for a reason.

Anna Berlanga, Transportation Alternatives’ Bronx and Uptown organizer, runs TA’s Open Exit 12N petition. This fall Felipe proposed a public walk, Anna made it an event with a flyer and a registration page, and Felipe brought in Cindy McLaughlin of Off-Ramps and a reporter from the Columbia Spectator. Open Plans co-hosted, and Andrea Fondulas walked with the group. On Sunday, November 22, thirty-one people registered for the walk, while Ira planted bulbs by the gate and invited passers-by to join.

We, Brook Constantz and Eric Bolton of Spherical Harmonics, arrived late. We found Ira’s website and TA’s petition in October 2026 and offered one more tool: a shared map anyone can draw on. We tested it in an online workshop on November 4, and after the walk we ran a workshop on it at Columbia’s Center for Smart Streetscapes. What the map shows goes to two Community Board 9 committees in December.
